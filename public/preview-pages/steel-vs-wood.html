MATERIAL COMPARISON

Steel vs. wood framing: compare the complete project.

Comparing framing systems means looking beyond the price of an individual stud. The practical decision considers framing materials, assembly details, labor, delivery, insulation strategy and the expected service environment.

Termite-resistant structural framing

Termites cannot consume galvanized steel framing members. This protects the steel components themselves from termite feeding, a useful distinction when evaluating long-term framing durability. Other wood-based and cellulose materials in a building still need appropriate protection.

What repairs can cost. In a 2024 U.S. survey of 600 homeowners, 94% of those who reported termite damage paid under $3,000 for repairs. The remaining 6% paid more. Over a home’s life, costs depend on repeat infestations and which parts need replacement. The survey measured repair bills over an unspecified period. Homeowner survey ↗

What strength loss looks like. In tests of termite-exposed hybrid-pine beams, the researchers’ trend line showed about 40% lower bending strength at 30% wood mass loss compared with zero loss. Tunnel location mattered, and individual results varied. A whole-home assessment requires inspection of the affected members and connections. Beam study, figure 10 ↗

Framing speed and labor

Steel members are dimensionally consistent, and some systems allow shop-built panels or cut-to-length components. Those approaches can reduce field work; the actual schedule depends on project complexity, fabrication and installer familiarity.

Cost comparisons that matter

Request equivalent scopes for stud sizes, joists, panels, connections, fasteners, sheathing interfaces, delivery, waste allowances and installation. This produces a more useful comparison than a per-stud price alone.
